Beliefs on Teaching and Learning

PRE-FIELD STUDY

My initial beliefs

Before my Field Study experience, I believed that teaching was primarily about explaining lessons clearly, providing accurate information, and helping learners understand the subject matter. I viewed the teacher as a guide who establishes classroom rules, maintains discipline, and ensures that learning objectives are achieved.

I also believed that learners needed encouragement, structured classroom routines, and appropriate learning materials to participate effectively. However, I still needed a deeper understanding of how different teaching strategies, learner motivation, and classroom environments influence students' learning experiences.

I entered my Field Study with the goal of observing real classroom practices and understanding how educational theories could be applied to teaching English.

POST-FIELD STUDY

How my beliefs developed

After my Field Study observations, I developed a broader understanding of teaching and learning. I realized that effective teaching involves more than presenting information. It also requires creating a conducive learning environment, understanding learner behavior, encouraging participation, and choosing teaching strategies that support different learning needs.

My observations showed that established classroom routines and verbal redirection could help maintain order without unnecessarily interrupting the lesson. I also observed how learners participated in group activities, recitation, and poetry interpretation, demonstrating the importance of collaboration and meaningful learning experiences.

I now believe that a learner-centered approach should be an important part of my teaching practice. I want learners to communicate, collaborate, interpret texts, express ideas, and apply what they have learned. I recognize that I must continue developing my classroom management, instructional planning, and reflective teaching skills.

Reflections on the Field Study Areas Observed

Each reflection follows three prompts: what I observed, what I learned, and how I developed.

Area 1 · School and Classroom Environment

What I observed

I observed organized seating, a whiteboard, a clock, a calendar, and poetry-related texts displayed on the walls. The teacher could rearrange seats into groups for collaborative activities. The school environment included a library, clinic and guidance office, accessible restrooms, and posted fire-exit information.

What I learned

The physical environment can support or hinder learning. Classroom organization, accessible resources, and appropriate seating can help learners focus, communicate, and participate. A conducive environment also involves safety, respectful interactions, and consistent routines.

How I developed

I became more attentive to the relationship between the learning environment and teaching effectiveness. As a future English teacher, I want to organize my classroom to support individual work, discussion, group activities, and engagement with reading materials.

Area 2 · Learner Behavior and Motivation

What I observed

I observed learners entering in an orderly manner, greeting the teacher, answering questions, taking notes, and raising their hands before reciting. Learners interacted during group activities. The worksheet records enthusiasm when grades were announced and notes that some learners talked with seatmates or changed seats during class.

What I learned

Learner behavior and motivation can be influenced by personal interest, recognition, teacher feedback, classroom conditions, and peer interaction. Group activities offer opportunities for communication and cooperation, while clear expectations can help maintain focus.

How I developed

I developed a greater appreciation for understanding learners before deciding how to respond to behavior. I want to encourage participation, recognize effort, establish clear goals, and create engaging activities. I also want to respond constructively to off-task behavior rather than assuming all learners have the same needs.

Area 3 · Teaching Strategies in Action

What I observed

I observed teacher-centered instruction during lesson introductions and discussions. Learners listened and participated in recitation. During application, learners worked in groups and applied their knowledge. The worksheet also records lecture-discussion, question-and-answer, demonstration, cooperative learning, and varied instructional materials.

What I learned

Different strategies serve different purposes. Teacher-centered instruction can provide explanations and structure, while learner-centered activities create opportunities for collaboration, application, and peer interaction. Effective lessons can combine approaches according to objectives and learners' needs.

How I developed

I want learners to do more than listen to explanations. As an English major, I want them to interpret literary texts, express opinions, participate in discussions, collaborate, and apply language skills in meaningful contexts. I also learned to prepare carefully so activities remain purposeful and aligned with lesson objectives.

Area 4 · Classroom Management Techniques

What I observed

I observed the teacher arranging the classroom, checking attendance, transitioning between activities, giving positive feedback after recitation, and verbally reminding learners to return their attention to ongoing tasks. At dismissal, learners were instructed to greet the teacher, arrange chairs, and pick up pieces of trash.

What I learned

Classroom management involves more than correcting misbehavior. Consistent routines, clear instructions, positive feedback, and timely redirection can help preserve learning time and maintain an orderly environment.

How I developed

I became more aware of balancing classroom structure with learner independence. I intend to establish consistent routines, communicate expectations clearly, and use constructive reminders. I also want learners to take responsibility for their learning and classroom environment while balancing teacher explanations with student participation.

Overall Reflection

My Field Study experience provided an opportunity to connect my academic preparation with actual classroom practices. Through my observations at Silliman University, particularly in Grade 7-Sikatuna, I gained a deeper understanding of the factors that contribute to an effective learning environment.

One important lesson I learned was that teaching requires careful planning, observation, and responsiveness. The physical arrangement of the classroom, routines established by the teacher, and strategies used to maintain order all contribute to the learning experience. I also observed that learners participate in different ways, including answering questions, taking notes, interacting with peers, and completing group activities.

My observations of teaching strategies helped me recognize the value of balancing direct instruction with learner-centered approaches. Although teacher explanations can provide structure and content knowledge, learners also need opportunities to communicate, collaborate, interpret ideas, and apply what they have learned. This is particularly important in English education, where language develops through meaningful opportunities to read, write, speak, listen, and interact.

I also learned that classroom management should not be limited to correcting disruptive behavior. Consistent routines, clear expectations, positive feedback, and appropriate redirection can help create an environment where learning continues productively. These practices must be combined with respect for learners and an understanding of their individual needs.

As a developing teacher, I recognize that I still need to improve my instructional planning, classroom management, assessment practices, and ability to facilitate student-centered learning. I want to become a teacher who creates engaging lessons, encourages critical thinking, and helps learners become more independent and confident.

Overall, this Field Study experience strengthened my commitment to becoming a reflective and responsible English teacher. Professional growth is continuous, and effective teaching requires learning from classroom experiences, evaluating my own practices, and making improvements based on evidence. I will carry these lessons into my future teaching practice as I work toward creating a supportive, inclusive, and meaningful learning environment for every learner.
